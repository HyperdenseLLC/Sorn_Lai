Threshold Syndrome

The White Forest boundary triggers immediate physiological rejection in unmodified humans. Soil samples show standard mineral composition, yet direct contact produces involuntary muscular recoil measurable at 0.3 seconds—faster than conscious processing. Pearl-white bark exhibits subcutaneous bioluminescence pulsing at 72-78 bpm, matching human resting heart rate. This synchronization may explain the discomfort: the forest attempts bio-rhythm matching while human systems resist integration.

Observable phenomenon: Holofly networks reorganize when humans enter detection range (approx. 15m). Light-thread tension redistributes to include new node, suggesting active environmental consciousness. Web recalculation takes 1.7 seconds regardless of swarm size, indicating distributed rather than centralized processing.

Falsifiable claim: Threshold rejection decreases proportionally with exposure time. Initial contact: 100% rejection rate. After 1 hour: 85%. After 6 hours: 60%. This suggests acclimatization rather than true resistance. Neural-hex readings show baseline corruption beginning at hour 3, supporting gradual integration hypothesis.
